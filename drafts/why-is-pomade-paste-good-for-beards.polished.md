## How do you apply Pomade Paste to a beard?
Use a small amount, warm it first, then work it in and brush it down. The whole routine takes a minute.

1. Scoop a dime-sized amount.
2. Warm it between your palms until it melts. The product page calls this waking up the botanicals.
3. Work it through the beard from root to tip, covering the cheeks, jaw and chin.
4. Brush it down with steady pressure to lay every strand without lifting.
5. Look at the line in the mirror and add a little more only where a stray hair still stands up.

Start small. You can always add a second layer; taking too much back out is harder.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much Pomade Paste should I use on my beard?
Start with a dime-sized amount, warmed between your palms until it melts. That covers most beards. If a few stray hairs still stand up after brushing, add a little more only on those spots. A light layer keeps the all-day hold without any weight, which is the point of a whipped paste.

### Does Pomade Paste leave a greasy feel?
No. The product page describes it as light, whipped and spongy, melting into an invisible hold with a clean, natural finish, with no grease and no cake. That is what makes it a good fit for a beard, which sits close to your skin all day and should look sharp rather than slick.

### Does Pomade Paste wash out?
Yes. Glory says it works instantly and washes out clean. Wash your face and beard as usual at the end of the day and the paste comes out with it, so you start fresh the next morning with a new, small amount and a quick brush to set the shape again.

### Do I need a brush to use Pomade Paste on a beard?
You can work it in with your fingers, but a brush gives a cleaner result. The Glory boar bristle brush lays every strand in one direction with steady pressure, spreads the paste evenly, and helps the beard line look sharp. The same brush also works for waves and fresh cuts.

### Can I use Pomade Paste on my hair and my beard at the same time?
Yes. Glory built it for beards, curls and waves, and says it also works for slick-backs and buns. Use the same warm-and-work-in routine for each: a small amount, warmed in your palms, worked through from root to tip, then brushed or finger-styled into place.
